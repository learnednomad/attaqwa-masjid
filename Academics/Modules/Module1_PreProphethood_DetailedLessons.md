## LESSON 1.1: ARABIA BEFORE ISLAM (3 Sessions)

### Session 1: Political and Geographic Context

#### **Learning Objectives**
- Understand the geographic significance of the Arabian Peninsula
- Analyze the political structure of pre-Islamic Arabia
- Recognize Allah's wisdom in choosing this location for the final message

#### **Opening (10 minutes)**
- **Du'a for beneficial knowledge**
- **Recitation**: Surah Quraysh (106) with brief tafsir from Baghawi
- **Essential Question**: "Why did Allah choose Arabia for His final message?"

#### **Main Content (35 minutes)**

##### **Part A: Geographic Setting (15 min)**
**Source: Sealed Nectar, Pages 17-25**
> "The Arabian Peninsula is enclosed in the west by the Red Sea and Sinai, in the east by the Arabian Gulf, in the south by the Arabian Sea... and in the north by old Syria and part of Iraq." 

**Key Points from Ibn Kathir (Al-Bidayah wan-Nihayah, Vol. 2, p. 180-185):**
- Strategic location between three continents
- Natural protection from foreign invasion
- Preservation of Arabic language purity
- Trade route significance

**Teaching Method:**
1. Display map of 6th century world
2. Mark major empires (Byzantine, Persian)
3. Highlight Arabia's unique position
4. Discuss language preservation

##### **Part B: Political Structure (20 min)**
**Source Citations:**

**From Sealed Nectar (p. 26-29):**
> "Rulership in Arabia was of two kinds: 1) Crowned kings who were not independence... 2) Heads of tribes and clans, who enjoyed the same authorities as kings..."

**Ibn Kathir's Analysis (Vol. 2, p. 186-190):**
- The Ghassanids (Syrian borders)
- The Lakhmids (Iraqi borders)  
- Yemen's various dynasties
- Makkah's unique position

**Baghawi's Commentary (Ma'alim at-Tanzil, Surah 106):**
- Quraysh's special status
- The sanctuary's political immunity
- Trade agreements and their significance

#### **Activity (10 minutes)**
**Primary Source Analysis:**
Students examine excerpts from:
1. Treaty of Ilaf (Quraysh trade agreements)
2. Poetry of Imru' al-Qays describing tribal politics
3. Compare with Quranic description in Surah Al-Rum (30:2-5)

#### **Assessment & Closing (5 minutes)**
- **Quick Check**: Name three political entities in pre-Islamic Arabia
- **Reflection Question**: How did political fragmentation prepare Arabia for Islam?
- **Homework**: Read Sealed Nectar pages 30-35

### Session 2: Religious Conditions

#### **Learning Objectives**
- Identify the religious practices of pre-Islamic Arabia
- Understand the concept of Hanifiyyah (Abrahamic monotheism)
- Recognize the corruption of the original message of Ibrahim عليه السلام

#### **Opening (10 minutes)**
- **Review**: Political map of Arabia
- **Recitation**: Surah Al-An'am (6:161) - Millat Ibrahim
- **Connection Question**: "What remained of Ibrahim's religion?"

#### **Main Content (35 minutes)**

##### **Part A: Idol Worship Development (15 min)**

**Sealed Nectar Account (p. 36-42):**
> "The Arabs still acknowledged Allah as the Supreme God but had associated partners with Him..."

**Ibn Kathir's Historical Narrative (Vol. 2, p. 195-200):**
- Story of Amr bin Luhay introducing idols
- The 360 idols around the Ka'bah
- Major idols: Lat, Uzza, Manat (Surah An-Najm 53:19-20)

**Authentication Note from Sahih Bukhari (#4623):**
نَبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ "رَأَيْتُ عَمْرَو بْنَ لُحَىٍّ يَجُرُّ قُصْبَهُ فِي النَّارِ"
"I saw 'Amr bin Luhay dragging his intestines in Hell..."

##### **Part B: The Hunafa (20 min)**

**Baghawi's Explanation (Sharh as-Sunnah, Vol. 13, p. 265):**
- Definition of Hanif
- Four famous Hunafa of Makkah:
  1. Waraqah ibn Nawfal
  2. Uthman ibn al-Huwayrith
  3. Ubaydullah ibn Jahsh
  4. Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl

**Detailed Account from Ibn Kathir (Vol. 2, p. 210-215):**
Story of Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl:
- His rejection of idolatry
- His search for truth
- His prayer at the Ka'bah
- The Prophet's ﷺ testimony about him

**Authentic Narration (Bukhari #3828):**
The Prophet ﷺ said about Zayd: "يُبْعَثُ أُمَّةً وَحْدَهُ" 
"He will be resurrected as a nation by himself."

#### **Critical Analysis Activity (10 minutes)**
Students evaluate three religious practices:
1. Tawaf (circumambulation) - Abrahamic origin
2. Animal sacrifice - Corrupted practice
3. Talbiyah - Compare pre-Islamic vs Islamic versions

**Pre-Islamic Talbiyah:**
"لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ إِلَّا شَرِيكًا هُوَ لَكَ تَمْلِكُهُ وَمَا مَلَكَ"

**Islamic Talbiyah:**
"لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ"

#### **Assessment & Closing (5 minutes)**
- **Comprehension Check**: List three corrupted religious practices
- **Critical Thinking**: Why did Allah preserve some Abrahamic practices?
- **Assignment**: Research one Hanif's story in detail

### Session 3: Social and Moral Conditions

#### **Learning Objectives**
- Analyze the social structure of pre-Islamic Arabian society
- Identify positive and negative aspects of Arab character
- Understand the necessity of prophetic guidance

#### **Opening (10 minutes)**
- **Du'a and Reflection**
- **Hadith Study** (Bukhari #3834):
"إِنَّمَا بُعِثْتُ لِأُتَمِّمَ مَكَارِمَ الْأَخْلَاقِ"
"I was sent to perfect noble character"
- **Discussion**: What needed perfecting?

#### **Main Content (35 minutes)**

##### **Part A: Social Evils (15 min)**

**Sealed Nectar Enumeration (p. 43-48):**
1. Female infanticide (Wa'd al-Banat)
2. Slavery and oppression
3. Usury (Riba)
4. Gambling (Maysir)
5. Alcohol consumption
6. Tribalism ('Asabiyyah)
7. Continuous warfare

**Quranic References with Baghawi's Tafsir:**
- Female infanticide: Surah At-Takwir (81:8-9)
- Tribalism: Surah Al-Hujurat (49:13)
- Intoxicants: Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:90)

**Ibn Kathir's Social Commentary (Vol. 2, p. 220-225):**
- The Days of the Arabs (Ayyam al-Arab) - tribal wars
- The Sacrilegious Wars (Harb al-Fijar)
- Blood feuds lasting generations

##### **Part B: Noble Qualities (10 min)**

**Balanced Perspective from Sources:**

**Sealed Nectar (p. 49-51):**
> "The Arabs had some commendable qualities:
> 1. Hospitality (Karam)
> 2. Keeping promises ('Ahd)
> 3. Sense of honor (Sharaf)
> 4. Bravery (Shuja'ah)
> 5. Eloquence (Fasahah)"

**Ibn Kathir's Examples (Vol. 2, p. 230-235):**
- Hatim at-Ta'i's legendary generosity
- The Hilf al-Fudul (Alliance of Virtue)
- Poetry as historical record

##### **Part C: Women's Status (10 min)**

**Critical Source Analysis:**

**Sealed Nectar (p. 47):**
- Property rights denied
- Inheritance exclusion
- Marriage without consent

**Balancing Evidence from Ibn Kathir:**
- Khadijah's رضي الله عنها business success
- Female poets and their influence
- Varied tribal practices

**Jurisprudential Note:**
Avoid overgeneralization - practices varied by tribe and region

#### **Interactive Exercise (10 minutes)**
**Scenario Analysis:**
Students receive cards with pre-Islamic practices and categorize them:
1. Completely rejected by Islam
2. Reformed and regulated by Islam
3. Affirmed and continued in Islam

Examples:
- Poetry → Reformed (halal content only)
- Pilgrimage → Reformed (purified from shirk)
- Female infanticide → Completely rejected

#### **Assessment & Closing (5 minutes)**
- **Evaluation**: Write three social reforms Islam would bring
- **Reflection**: How does understanding jahiliyyah help us appreciate Islam?
- **Preview**: Next lesson - The Noble Lineage

## LESSON 1.2: THE PROPHET'S LINEAGE & THE YEAR OF THE ELEPHANT

### Session 1: The Noble Lineage (Detailed Genealogy Study)

#### **Learning Objectives**
- Trace the Prophet's ﷺ lineage to Ibrahim عليه السلام
- Understand the significance of his noble ancestry
- Recognize Divine preservation of the prophetic line

#### **Opening (10 minutes)**
- **Quranic Foundation**: Surah Ash-Shu'ara (26:219)
"وَتَقَلُّبَكَ فِي السَّاجِدِينَ"
"And your movement among those who prostrate"
- **Baghawi's Interpretation**: The Prophet's ﷺ transfer through believing ancestors

#### **Main Content (40 minutes)**

##### **Part A: The Authenticated Lineage (20 min)**

**Complete Chain from Sealed Nectar (p. 63-65):**

**To Adnan (Agreed Upon by All Scholars):**
محمد بن عبد الله بن عبد المطلب بن هاشم بن عبد مناف بن قصي بن كلاب بن مرة بن كعب بن لؤي بن غالب بن فهر بن مالك بن النضر بن كنانة بن خزيمة بن مدركة بن إلياس بن مضر بن نزار بن معد بن عدنان

**Ibn Kathir's Authentication (Vol. 2, p. 240-245):**
- Consensus on lineage to Adnan
- Disagreement beyond Adnan
- The Prophet's ﷺ own limitation

**Hadith Evidence (Sahih - Ibn Sa'd):**
The Prophet ﷺ said: "لا تجاوزوا معد بن عدنان"
"Do not go beyond Ma'add ibn Adnan"

##### **Part B: Significance of Key Ancestors (20 min)**

**1. Qusayy ibn Kilab:**
- Unified Quraysh in Makkah
- Established Dar an-Nadwah
- Organized pilgrimage services

**2. Hashim ibn Abd Manaf:**
- Initiated Ilaf (trade agreements)
- Fed pilgrims (establishing siqayah and rifadah)
- Father of Abd al-Muttalib

**3. Abd al-Muttalib:**
- Rediscovery of Zamzam
- Vow regarding his sons
- Defense against Abraha

**Ibn Kathir's Critical Analysis (Vol. 2, p. 250-260):**
- Each ancestor's role in preserving monotheistic elements
- Divine protection of the lineage
- Prophetic signs in the ancestry

#### **Genealogical Chart Activity (10 minutes)**
Students create visual lineage charts showing:
1. Direct line to Adnan
2. Connection to Ismail عليه السلام
3. Branch points to other Arab tribes

#### **Assessment**
- Memorization of lineage to Hashim (minimum)
- Understanding of each ancestor's contribution
- Recognition of Divine preservation

### Session 2: The Year of the Elephant - Detailed Analysis

#### **Learning Objectives**
- Analyze the historical and religious significance of the event
- Understand Divine protection of the Ka'bah
- Connect to the Prophet's ﷺ birth year

#### **Opening (10 minutes)**
- **Recitation**: Complete Surah Al-Fil with tajweed
- **Immediate Context**: Why is this event so significant?

#### **Main Content (40 minutes)**

##### **Part A: Historical Background (15 min)**

**Sealed Nectar Account (p. 55-62):**
- Abraha's construction of al-Qullays
- His motivation to divert pilgrimage
- The incident that triggered invasion

**Ibn Kathir's Detailed Narrative (Vol. 2, p. 265-275):**
1. **The Yemeni Context:**
   - Abyssinian conquest of Yemen
   - Abraha's rise to power
   - Economic motivations

2. **The March to Makkah:**
   - Army composition (60,000 soldiers)
   - The elephant Mahmud
   - Arab tribes' responses

**Baghawi's Addition (Ma'alim at-Tanzil, Surah 105):**
- The dialogue between Abraha and Abd al-Muttalib
- The famous statement: "لِلْبَيْتِ رَبٌّ يَحْمِيهِ"
  "The House has a Lord who will protect it"

##### **Part B: The Miracle Itself (15 min)**

**Multiple Source Analysis:**

**Quranic Description (Surah Al-Fil):**
أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصْحَابِ الْفِيلِ

**Ibn Kathir's Compilation of Eyewitness Accounts:**
- The elephant refusing to move toward Ka'bah
- The appearance of the birds (Ababil)
- The stones of Sijjil
- The army's destruction

**Critical Hadith Analysis:**
Various narrations about survivors who later accepted Islam
- Their testimony about the event
- Physical evidence that remained

##### **Part C: Significance and Timing (10 min)**

**Connection to Prophet's Birth:**

**Sealed Nectar's Chronology (p. 63):**
- Born 50-55 days after the event (most accurate opinion)
- Monday, 12th Rabi' al-Awwal
- Year 571 CE (approximate)

**Ibn Kathir's Theological Analysis:**
1. Preparation for Prophethood
2. Establishing Arabia's recognition of Divine power
3. Preserving the Ka'bah for its destined role

#### **Primary Source Workshop (10 minutes)**
Students analyze:
1. Pre-Islamic poetry about the event
2. Christian sources mentioning the failed invasion
3. Archaeological considerations

#### **Assessment & Application**
- Essay: "Divine Protection: From Abraha to the Present"
- Discussion: How does this event demonstrate Allah's planning?
- Memorization: Surah Al-Fil with understanding

## LESSON 1.3: THE PROPHET'S BIRTH & EARLY CHILDHOOD

### Session 1: The Blessed Birth - Authenticated Accounts

#### **Learning Objectives**
- Establish authenticated facts about the Prophet's ﷺ birth
- Distinguish between authentic narrations and later additions
- Understand the circumstances of his blessed birth

#### **Opening (10 minutes)**
- **Hadith**: "أنا دعوة أبي إبراهيم وبشارة عيسى"
  "I am the prayer of my father Ibrahim and the glad tidings of Isa"
- **Context Setting**: The world awaiting the final Prophet

#### **Main Content (40 minutes)**

##### **Part A: Authenticated Birth Details (20 min)**

**What is Definitively Established:**

**From Sealed Nectar (p. 63-67):**
1. **Date**: Monday, Rabi' al-Awwal
   - 12th (majority opinion)
   - 9th (some scholars)
   - Year of the Elephant

2. **Parents**:
   - Father: Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib
   - Mother: Aminah bint Wahb
   - Father died before birth

3. **Location**: Shi'b Bani Hashim, Makkah

**Ibn Kathir's Critical Approach (Vol. 2, p. 280-285):**
- Rejects many claimed "miracles" at birth
- Focuses on authenticated narrations only
- Analysis of various historical claims

**Baghawi's Balanced Presentation:**
- The light seen by his mother (يخرج منها نور)
- Authentic vs. embellished accounts
- Scholarly consensus points

##### **Part B: Family Circumstances (20 min)**

**Abdullah's Death:**

**Sealed Nectar Details (p. 64):**
- Trade journey to Syria
- Illness in Yathrib
- Death at age 25
- Minimal inheritance

**Aminah's Situation:**
- Young widow (approximately 20)
- Under protection of Abd al-Muttalib
- Noble lineage from Bani Zuhrah

**The Naming:**
- Named "Muhammad" (unprecedented in Arab tradition)
- Abd al-Muttalib's inspiration
- Meaning and significance

**Authentic Narration about the Name:**
"سَمَّيْتُهُ مُحَمَّدًا لِيُحْمَدَ فِي السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ"

#### **Critical Analysis Exercise (10 minutes)**
Compare three biographical sources:
1. What all sources agree upon
2. What some sources mention
3. What appears to be later additions

#### **Assessment**
- List five authenticated facts about the birth
- Explain the jurisprudential approach to birth narrations
- Understand the importance of authentic sources

### Session 2: Foster Care with Halimah as-Sa'diyyah

#### **Learning Objectives**
- Understand the Arab custom of desert fosterage
- Learn about the Prophet's ﷺ time with Banu Sa'd
- Analyze the authentic accounts of this period

#### **Opening (10 minutes)**
- **Cultural Context**: Why desert fosterage?
- **Benefits**: Pure Arabic, healthy environment, character building

#### **Main Content (40 minutes)**

##### **Part A: The Selection Process (15 min)**

**Sealed Nectar Account (p. 68-70):**
- Arrival of Banu Sa'd wet nurses
- Halimah's initial reluctance (orphan status)
- The blessed acceptance

**Halimah's Own Narration (Ibn Kathir, Vol. 2, p. 290-295):**
- The journey to Makkah (difficult year)
- Immediate blessings noticed
- Physical changes in livestock
- Prosperity during his stay

**Critical Note on Authentication:**
- Narration through Ibn Ishaq
- Some scholars' reservations
- Core elements accepted

##### **Part B: Life in the Desert (15 min)**

**Authenticated Elements:**
1. Duration: Approximately 4-5 years
2. Location: Banu Sa'd territory
3. Foster siblings: Shayma and others
4. Language: Pure Arabic acquisition

**The Splitting of the Chest Incident:**

**Multiple Source Analysis:**
- Quranic reference: Surah Ash-Sharh (94:1)
- Authentic hadith in Muslim
- Age at occurrence: 4 years
- Halimah's concern and return to Aminah

**Ibn Kathir's Scholarly Treatment:**
- Physical vs. spiritual interpretations
- Similar incidents later in life
- Theological significance

##### **Part C: Return to Mother (10 min)**

**Sequence of Events:**
- Return after chest incident
- Time with mother Aminah (age 4-6)
- Visit to father's grave in Yathrib
- Aminah's death at Abwa'
- Umm Ayman's care and return to Makkah

**Emotional and Psychological Aspects:**
- Early experiences with loss
- Development of empathy
- Preparation for prophetic mission

#### **Reflective Activity (10 minutes)**
Students discuss:
1. Wisdom in the desert upbringing
2. Early character formation
3. Divine protection throughout childhood

## COMPREHENSIVE MODULE 1 ASSESSMENT MATERIALS

### Formative Assessments

#### **Daily Exit Tickets**
1. Name three political entities in pre-Islamic Arabia
2. Define "Hanif" and name two examples
3. List the authenticated facts about the Prophet's ﷺ birth
4. Explain the significance of the Year of the Elephant

#### **Weekly Quizzes**

**Week 1 Quiz: Pre-Islamic Arabia**
1. Multiple Choice (5 questions) - Political structures
2. Short Answer (3 questions) - Religious practices
3. Essay (1 question) - Analyze the need for prophetic guidance

**Week 2 Quiz: Lineage and Birth**
1. Genealogy completion - Fill in the lineage chart
2. True/False with corrections - Birth narrations
3. Source analysis - Identify authentic vs. weak narrations

### Summative Assessment

#### **Module 1 Examination (100 points)**

**Section A: Knowledge (30 points)**
- 15 Multiple choice questions
- Focus on facts from three primary sources

**Section B: Understanding (30 points)**
- 5 Short answer questions
- Explain concepts and connections

**Section C: Application (20 points)**
- 2 Scenario analyses
- Apply learning to contemporary contexts

**Section D: Critical Thinking (20 points)**
- 1 Essay question
- Compare sources and evaluate authenticity

### Project-Based Assessment

#### **Option 1: Research Project**
"The Preservation of Monotheism in Pre-Islamic Arabia"
- Use all three primary sources
- 1500-word paper
- Proper citations required

#### **Option 2: Creative Presentation**
"A Day in Pre-Islamic Makkah"
- Historically accurate depiction
- Include political, social, and religious elements
- Present to class with Q&A

#### **Option 3: Source Comparison**
Compare treatment of one topic across all three sources
- Create comparison chart
- Analyze differences in approach
- Present findings

### Rubrics

#### **Essay Rubric (20 points)**
- **Use of Sources (5 pts)**: Accurate citations from primary texts
- **Authentication (5 pts)**: Distinguishes strong/weak narrations
- **Analysis (5 pts)**: Deep understanding demonstrated
- **Application (5 pts)**: Relevant contemporary connections

#### **Project Rubric (100 points)**
- **Research Quality (25 pts)**: Use of primary sources
- **Accuracy (25 pts)**: Historically/religiously accurate
- **Analysis (25 pts)**: Critical thinking evident
- **Presentation (25 pts)**: Clear, organized, engaging
